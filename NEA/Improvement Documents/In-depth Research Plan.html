2.1

Benefits of robotics education

Outcomes
3× more likely to show increased STEM interest vs. non-participants (Brandeis/FIRST, 2024)
g=0.57 positive effect size for learning outcomes across meta-analysis of 60+ studies (Springer, 2024)
59% of female FIRST alumni more likely to declare engineering/CS majors (Brandeis 10yr)
Robotics embeds the full engineering design cycle — identify, plan, build, test, iterate — directly teaching growth mindset by design.
Cross-disciplinary: one robot build requires physics (torque/gear ratios), maths (control loops), CS (programming logic), and engineering (structure).
10-week intervention study (60 students, 2024): experimental group showed significant improvement vs. traditional instruction across all assessment metrics.
Sources: Brandeis 10yr Study 2024; Springer Nature Meta-Analysis 2024; Zhang et al. 2024

Problems with robotics education

Barriers
26.7% of teachers have integrated robots into teaching — leaving 73.3% without adoption (Market.us survey, 2026)
15% of US schools lack sufficient resources for robotics integration (NCES data)
Top barriers cited by educators
Cost of hardware
92%
Teacher training
81%
Curriculum fit
67%
Maintenance
54%
Rapid obsolescence: platforms become outdated within 3–4 years, requiring costly repurchase cycles.
Gender gap: documented lower self-identification as "robotic learners" among girls without targeted intervention programmes.
Sources: Market.us 2026; NCES; AI for Good / ITU 2024; RobotLab 2019
2.3

Target user data

Primary research
88% of surveyed Yr9–10 students expressed interest in robotics if perceived as accessible (n=40, Doha, 2025)
73% had never built a physical electronic circuit before
2.1/5 mean teacher confidence rating for microcontroller programming (interviewed n=8 teachers)
Target: students aged 11–16, British/international curriculum, Gulf region context.
Budget constraint: school allocation = £15–£50 per student for enrichment tech resources.
61% of surveyed students had written fewer than 20 lines of original code — platform must scaffold from zero.
Sources: Primary survey, Doha international school, 2025; teacher interviews

2.5

Classroom limitations

Constraints
50–60 minute standard lesson vs. 90–120 min needed for a full design-build-test cycle
28% projected annual growth in school robotics spend 2018–2023, despite persistent classroom delivery failures (RobotLab)
IT network restrictions in managed school environments block firmware upload tools, GitHub, and common IDEs — browser-based programming is essential.
H&S regulations in Qatar schools prohibit soldering irons and Li-Po batteries without specialist facilities.
Shared equipment pools prevent individual student access between lessons, breaking the iterative learning loop.
Open-plan school environments create conflict: robot movement and noise disturb adjacent lessons.
Sources: RobotLab/eSchoolNews 2019; AI for Good ITU 2024; QMoE H&S guidelines
